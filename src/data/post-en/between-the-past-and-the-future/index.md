Following the thread of my previous post about how the change brought by AI is inevitable and, practically, you either adapt or get left behind, these past few weeks I've been working 100% with Claude Code as my programming partner. Both my wife and I use it day to day, and part of our conversations revolve around how AI helps us. On one hand, it frees us from those tedious, repetitive tasks; on the other, it gives us that false feeling of being experts at something.

### Do I really know what I'm doing?

In my case, I'm using Claude Code to optimize my old code, find bugs, improve readability and review security issues, but I try to keep everything I do within certain limits. Maybe I could take it much further and let it move on its own, but I like knowing that the instructions I give have a particular reason behind them, and aren't something as vague as "make it better", "optimize this" or "make it more secure". Otherwise I'd end up delegating a big part of who I am to an external system.

### Don't make me think

Until today, I've always tried to work under one philosophy: I don't want to think about which program to open or what I need to set up in order to work. I want an experience similar to driving, where my brain doesn't think about which pedal to press or which gear to shift to, but simply "knows".

Something similar is happening to me with Claude Code. I don't want to have to think about how to do certain things. I want to set the rules clearly from the start, so that when certain situations come up, the AI knows what to do, when to do it and, if not, asks me which actions to take. Otherwise I'd end up doing exactly what I want to avoid: having to think about what my next step is.

It's a bit funny where we're heading. On one hand we have the possibility of delegating many things to AI, but on the other we don't want to feel useless to the point where there's no need for us to think about anything. That's where I find myself, between what I used to do that made me feel useful and what I can do now at the risk of feeling useless, and it's a balance I'm trying to find.
