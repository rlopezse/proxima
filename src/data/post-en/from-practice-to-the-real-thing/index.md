When you learn something new, you tend to do it in a controlled environment, at your own pace. There's plenty of time, right? You picture that moment and everything is orderly: you go about it without pressure, without stress, and everything works. But sometimes life pushes you into situations you hadn't planned for and forces you to use what you learned before you feel ready.

That's exactly what happened to me this week. My idea with Arch was always to practice on my own time, every once in a while, no rush. At work I use a Mac set up 100%: shortcuts, access, everything in its place. But this week, for family reasons, I had to leave the Mac at the office and rely entirely on Arch to work. And I wasn't ready.

### From the bench to starting the game

I didn't even have accents configured, or notifications, nothing. It was like going out to play a match without warming up and without knowing the lineup: you only know the basics and you have to improvise. That's what I did.

Between configuring things on the fly and forcing myself to learn how to use it day to day, those three days felt like I'd been practicing my whole life. Now I feel like I can use it without a problem, like a second computer. It still has its quirks — Hyprland arranges windows however it feels like, the Wi-Fi drops whenever it wants — but beyond that, I reached a point where I'm no longer afraid of whatever might happen. The system and I operate together, not with the comfort of a Mac, but enough that I no longer depend 100% on my original machine.

### Learning on the fly

And that's how I understood that it's often better to learn in the moment than to follow tutorials or guidelines. Do, break, reconfigure. You can simulate those environments and push yourself further, but when circumstances force your hand, no tutorial or book beats actually learning for real.
